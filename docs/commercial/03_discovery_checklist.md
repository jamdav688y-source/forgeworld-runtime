# Discovery Conversation Checklist

> **DRAFT FOR OPERATOR USE.** Use this in the free discovery conversation (Option A). Record answers in the customer's own words where possible, and note who said what. Do **not** record names of the customer's clients, personal details or credentials.

## Before the call
- [ ] The prospect has agreed to a conversation about an operational problem. Note when and how.
- [ ] You have explained that notes will be taken, and asked whether that is acceptable.
- [ ] You have *not* promised any outcome, price or timeline.

## 1. The problem in their words
- [ ] "Where does work go missing, wait, or get done twice?" Write down the answer verbatim.
- [ ] "Can you give me a recent example?" Get one concrete instance, not a generalisation.
- [ ] "How do you know it happens?" Is it something seen, measured, reported by customers, or a feeling?

## 2. Is it one workflow we can bound?
- [ ] Which workflow? Name where it starts and where it ends.
- [ ] Which team or location runs it?
- [ ] Roughly how many items go through it per week or month? *(the customer's estimate; record it as theirs)*
- [ ] Which tools hold the data (spreadsheet, ticketing, job management, paper)? Can they export it **without personal details**?

## 3. Is it economically meaningful?
*Record every answer as the customer's own estimate. These are not findings.*
- [ ] What does one lost or delayed item cost them? Lost sale, rework hours, penalty, customer complaint?
- [ ] How often does it happen?
- [ ] Who notices, and what do they do about it today?
- [ ] Have they tried to fix it before? What happened?
- [ ] If this were solved, what would be different in three months?

## 4. Fit and readiness
- [ ] Who would own the diagnostic on their side (process owner)?
- [ ] Can up to five staff spare about 45 minutes each over three weeks?
- [ ] Who decides on spending, and is a budget of this size plausible? *(Do not quote a price unless the operator has approved it.)*
- [ ] Any constraints: regulated data, union or works-council rules, confidentiality obligations?

## 5. Close the call
- [ ] Summarise the problem back in one sentence and ask "Is that right?"
- [ ] Agree whether a written proposal makes sense. Do not send one without operator approval.
- [ ] Note any requested follow-up and its date.

## Red flags — pause and consult the operator
- The problem is really about an individual's performance.
- Solving it would require access to sensitive personal, health or financial data.
- They expect guaranteed savings or a software build within the diagnostic.
- They want the work done without their staff's knowledge.
